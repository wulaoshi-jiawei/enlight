Hong Kong

Hong Kong is a harbour city in the full sense: a mountain at its back, the water in front, and one of the great skyline views in Asia. Ferries still cross Victoria Harbour as they always have; above them, the towers of the Island rise against the Peak, and at evening the ridges light up as if the city were a single room. It is a place of markets and tramlines, of quiet temples tucked behind glass façades, of Cantonese kitchens that have been feeding the same streets for decades, and of a harbour that has never stopped working.

For travellers coming from Southeast Asia, Australia and the Pacific, it is a natural first landing, and a well-worn way onto the mainland. It is a beginning at the edge of the country, with the road still leading inward, toward Wudang.

When to go & weather

Autumn is Hong Kong’s finest season: clear, dry days and the harbour at its sharpest. Spring can be humid and misty; summer is hot, with the chance of typhoons; winter is mild and, for many travellers, surprisingly pleasant.

Travel Guides

How to get there

By air

Hong Kong International Airport (HKG) is one of Asia’s great hubs, with connections across the Pacific, Australia, Southeast Asia and the rest of China. The Airport Express reaches the city in about half an hour; taxis and buses cover the rest.

By train and ferry

High-speed rail from West Kowloon runs to Shenzhen, Guangzhou and onward into the mainland network. Ferries still serve Macau and a number of Pearl River ports — a reminder that this has always been a city reached by water as much as by air.

Getting around

The MTR is fast and almost unfailingly clear. Trams still rattle along the north shore of the Island; the Star Ferry remains the most beautiful short crossing in the city. Taxis are plentiful. For the Peak, the tram is part of the visit; for the outlying islands, the ferry is the whole point.

Best time to visit

October to December is the season most people hope for: low humidity, cooler evenings, and views from the Peak that can stretch for miles. January and February are mild. From March the air grows heavier; May to September are hot and wet, and typhoons are possible, especially in late summer. If you come then, keep a flexible day in hand.

Places Of Interest

Victoria Harbour and the Star Ferry

The harbour is still the reason the city exists, and the Star Ferry is still the best way to see it: a few minutes on the water, the Island rising on one side, Kowloon on the other, and the sense that this has always been a working port, not merely a view.

The Peak

Above the towers, the Peak looks down on the harbour and, on a clear day, out toward the outlying islands. Go late in the afternoon and stay for the lights; the walk around the ridge is as good as the lookout itself.

Markets, temples and the older streets

Behind the glass, Hong Kong is still a city of markets and small temples, of dai pai dong and tram stops, of neighbourhoods that have kept their own hours. A morning in one of them will tell you more than another hour among the towers.

The outlying islands

A short ferry from the centre, islands such as Lamma and Cheung Chau still move at a different pace: village streets, a walk along the coast, and a seafood dinner before the boat back. They are a reminder that Hong Kong has always been more than its skyline.

Hong Kong is a beginning at the edge of the country. From here, the road still leads inward, toward Wudang, and toward the rest of China.
