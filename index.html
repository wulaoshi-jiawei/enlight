Brief Introduction

Beijing

Beijing is our door to the north. Here the older capital and the present one still share the same day: palaces and temples, wide squares and their public life, hutongs and teahouses, and the newer streets of galleries and shops. Close at hand are Chengde, Datong and Pingyao, a lesser-known China within easy reach of the capital. Follow the Great Wall east and it meets the sea at Shanhaiguan; farther on, in the forests of Jilin, Changbai Mountain rises to Heaven Lake.

Shanghai

Shanghai is the international city we call home, and one of our doors to the east. It is a harbour of long waterfronts and a skyline that still faces the world, a city of trade and of contemporary life, open to the river and to the sea.

Hangzhou

Hangzhou is as much a place to stay as a place to arrive. West Lake and the tea hills sit at the heart of the city, a quieter and more watery China of gardens, temple hills, and the green country of Zhejiang.

Xi’an

Xi’an is the natural beginning in the west: an ancient capital, and the starting point of the Silk Road in China. From here the corridor runs through Tianshui, Lanzhou and Zhangye, on to Jiayuguan, Dunhuang and Yumen Pass, and the long road west. The way into Shaanxi also comes first to Ankang, a quieter stop on the approach to the city. Turn south from Xi’an and the road leads toward Chengdu and Chongqing — one a city of tea houses and unhurried meals, the other a river city built up the hills.

Xiamen

Xiamen is a sea city on the Fujian coast, and one of our doors to the south. The island of Gulangyu sits just across the water, and the mood here is of a harbour city — sea light, old lanes, and a long habit of looking outward.

Hong Kong

Hong Kong remains a natural arrival from the Pacific — a working harbour as much as a skyline, and a city of trade and of passage, set between the sea and the steep green hills.
