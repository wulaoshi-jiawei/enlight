A port, and a place to linger

Hangzhou has been written about for a thousand years, and West Lake is the reason. A pagoda on the wooded shore, boats moving slowly on the water, willows along the causeways, and the Longjing tea hills just beyond the city — this is the landscape Chinese poets and painters kept returning to. It is still a living city: a place of silk and tea, of university campuses and quiet temples, and of evenings when the lake holds the last of the light.

It is one of the six ports of entry we use, and an easy city to reach. Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport has domestic and a growing number of international flights; Hangzhou East is about an hour by high-speed rail from Shanghai Hongqiao, and well connected to Beijing, Nanjing and the rest of the network. There are also direct flights from Hangzhou to Wudangshan Airport in Shiyan, a little over two hours in the air, which makes the city a natural beginning if the journey continues to Wudang Mountain.
